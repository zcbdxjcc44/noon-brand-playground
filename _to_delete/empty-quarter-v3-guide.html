EMPTY QUARTER · V3 · CHANGE SPEC + APPLICATIONS

What changes in the guide, and what it looks like applied

v4 is not wrong, it is inverted. It took a retro-futurist source built on a luminous sky with sandstone structure and terracotta framing, and made the framing colour the field. Everything below is the smallest set of changes that puts the source's logic back, plus the three surfaces it has to survive: a subject-agnostic slide cover, the presenter stage view, and the student journey page. No new hexes — every value is from the locked ten, the Tracks families, or the approved cover material.

01 · The seven changes

Everything else in v4 stays. These are the elements that have to move.

#ELEMENTV4 TODAYV3
1The fieldSaturated terracotta holds the ground on covers and ceremonial pages. A graded sky holds the ground. Terracotta drops to ≤12%, used as frame and heat.
2AtmosphereFlat fills. No light source anywhere in the system. One sun, three planes. Aerial perspective: distant planes lighter and flatter, near planes darker.
3FacetingA pattern swatch applied as surface texture. A construction grammar — how every form is built. Planes, value steps, hard edges, one light direction.
4MovementDune waves. Geological, static, going nowhere. The ribbon — one flowing route per view, carrying progress and journey.
5Subject identityCarried by full-bleed family colour. Carried by the facet tint, rail and chip. The ground stays neutral so one background serves every subject.
6CaptionsCaps mono at 0.18–0.22em tracking on every label. Mono is for numerals only. Labels become sentence-case Hanken. This is the biggest age tell in the system.
7VoiceSurveyor's field notebook — "specimen", "closed transmission". Plain and warm. Keep "route" and "journey"; lose the affectation.
What is deliberately not changing. The v10 grain edge recipe, the Option D slim band and occlusion, the khatam and pinboard patterns, the foil seam, the noon Tracks lockup, Hanken Grotesk + IBM Plex Sans Arabic, and the 60/30/10 ratio discipline on working pages. v3 is a re-weighting, not a redraw.

02 · The sky — atmosphere, never scenery

The sky is the new field, and it is abstract by rule. It carries no content of its own, because it has to sit behind chemistry and behind poetry without favouring either. Anything recognisable — a city, a dune ridge, a landmark — belongs to the subject illustration, not the ground.

S1

Three stops, one direction

Deeper above, palest at the horizon. #DDCAA6 → #EFE6D4 → #F8F3E9. Never a flat fill, never a radial.

S2

One sun, and it is pale

A soft core no larger than 80px on a 1080 stage, with a wide low-opacity bloom. It is a light source, not a graphic. One per view.

S3

The horizon sits at 55–62%

Below it, one terrace plane a value step darker than the sand material, so sand-bodied forms separate from the ground.

S4

Cool lives in the haze

Water #7C99B4 at 15–30%, banded at the horizon only. It is atmospheric shadow. It never becomes a fill, a chip, or a brand colour.

S5

Nothing else in the ground

Sky, horizon, terrace, terracotta frame. That is the whole background. If a cover needs more, the illustration provides it.

Applied — the same ground under four subjects

Identical background in all four. Only the facet tint, the rail and the chip change.

03 · Faceting as construction

In the source illustration every rock, tower and ridge is built the same way: a small number of flat planes meeting at hard edges, with two or three value steps and a single light direction. That is a spec, and it is what makes the style read as designed-in-1994-about-2050 rather than merely textured. It governs icons, covers, illustration, empty states and the monument moment.

F1

3 to 7 planes

Fewer than three and it is a shape; more than seven and it is noise. Slivers below 12° are not planes — merge them.

F2

Two or three value steps

Family light on the lit plane, family core on the body, family deep in shadow. Steps 6–10% lightness apart. Never four.

F3

Hard edges, no outline

Planes meet directly. No strokes, no gradients inside a plane, no soft shadow between them.

F4

One light, declared

Upper-left by default, and it must agree with the sun in the sky. Every form on the screen is lit from the same side.

F5

Grain at the edge only

The approved v10 edge recipe still applies to sand-bodied forms. Flat core, fine dense grain at the silhouette, no blur, no halo.

04 · Terracotta's new job, and the ribbon

Terracotta is not reduced in importance — it is given a role it can actually hold. As a field it was wallpaper. As a frame and as heat it becomes the thing that identifies the brand at a glance and the thing that signals urgency.

The ribbon

One flowing route per view, with a lighter leading edge. At poster scale it is a 46px band with an 8px highlight; in UI it collapses to a 4px spine with a 2px highlight and becomes the journey's backbone. It replaces the dune wave wherever the dune was standing in for motion.

06 · Application A — presenter stage view

Dark stays presenter-only, per the depth grammar. v3 does not make the presenter light; it gives the dark the same atmosphere the covers get. The night sky is graded the same way — one light source, low on the horizon, warm — and the cool haze does the same work it does in daylight. Terracotta appears as heat at the frame, and the ribbon becomes the route the classroom cameras sit on.

  • Night sky, one light. Ink at the top grading to a warm afterglow at the horizon — the same three-stop rule, inverted.
  • Cameras as waypoints on the ribbon. The route replaces the scattered map; position on it means order of speaking.
  • Coral rim, unchanged. Active speaker keeps its reserved signal and its 3s breath.
  • Mono for numerals only. The status strip now reads Live · 47:32 · Slide 4 of 12.
  • Ember at the frame. Heat sits at the edge, where terracotta belongs.
  • Faceted horizon. Low-contrast, three planes, no scenery — it is atmosphere, not a landscape.

07 · Application B — student journey

The prototype is the system's hardest test: dense, functional, mobile, and currently generic. Nothing here needs illustration. The whole v3 story is carried by a plaster ground, a sky band at the head, the ribbon as the spine, and faceted waypoints instead of circles.

  • The spine is the ribbon. Completed run in noon green, current at the waypoint, upcoming dashed. One route, top to bottom.
  • Waypoints are faceted, not round. Three planes, one light direction, tinted by state — the same construction rule as the covers, at 32px.
  • Ember carries "behind". Heat is the role terracotta was given in §04, so the alert needs no red borrowed from outside the palette.
  • Water marks the current unit. Progression is water's job in the locked ten; the generic system blue is gone.
  • Sky band at the head. A 180px gradient with a hairline horizon. Enough atmosphere to place the screen in the world, no illustration to fight the content.
  • Mono only on numerals. 24 days, 2 off track, 7 more units. Labels are sentence-case Hanken.
  • Strikethrough is gone. Completed units carry a green waypoint and muted ink — striking out a finished unit reads as cancelled, not achieved.

08 · Don'ts

No scenery in the ground · no second sun · no light source disagreeing with the facet shading · no four-step facets · no terracotta above 12% of a surface · no cool as a fill · no more than one ribbon per view · no caps mono on a word that is not a number · no full-bleed family core on ceremonial pages · no strikethrough on completed work · no ember as decoration.
