The two places the sweep actually bites

The countdown is where removing caps-mono costs something, because the label is doing real typographic work against the foil numeral. Three treatments below, control first. The journey page is where the cover recipe has to be applied with restraint — full-bleed colour and a big motif would wreck a dense list, so only the ambient lattice, the punctuation marks and the swipe come across.

02 · Student journey — corrected

No sky, no horizon, no ribbon, no faceted rock. What carries over from the cover recipe is the ambient lattice at 4% behind the header only, the punctuation marks in dead space, and the swipe as an emphasis device. State markers use the sphere-and-orbit grammar at 30px — a flat circle cut by one chord, with a thin ring over it.

  • Subject colour drives the screen. Quant is the sky family, so the active tab, the current unit and its marker all read #86A7BE / #5C7E96. The generic system blue is gone, and switching tabs re-tints the page.
  • Ring lattice at 4% behind the header — the same layer as the cover, at a sixth of the strength. Enough to say Tracks, not enough to compete with a list.
  • Markers follow the liked grammar. Flat circle, one straight chord, light above and deep below, thin cream ring over the top. No modelling, no facets.
  • Punctuation. One spark by the header, one small plus low in the empty run before the nav. Same job as on the cover.
  • Swipe on the count. "2 off track" gets the family-light swipe rather than a red pill — emphasis borrowed from the cover instead of an alert colour borrowed from outside the palette.
  • Ember for behind, not red. #E8663A is the palette's heat role.
  • Labels sentence-case, numerals mono. "Unit 1", "Coming later" in Hanken; 24, 2, 7 in JetBrains.
  • No strikethrough. Completed units read as done through the marker and muted ink.
What actually happened, against what I predicted. I expected C to go bottom-heavy with the standing label gone. It did not — the numeral holds the upper third on its own and the single sentence reads calmer than B. B is the weakest of the three, because it keeps the two-part structure but fills it with two soft lines instead of one firm one: "Class begins in" above and "seconds" below both sit at low contrast and neither anchors. If the goal is dropping caps-mono, C is the cleaner move and A is the honest fallback. On the journey, the header lattice is the risky element — it is at 6% here and nearly invisible in a screenshot, but it may bloom on a real OLED phone at full brightness. Worth checking on device before committing.
