The night guides

Dark sky is where wonder and way-finding live: heroes, milestones, progression maps. Gold is the voice that points the way — drawn as line, spent sparingly.

The paper carries

Reading, answering and doing happen on chalky plaster paper. Four textured pastels classify content — coral, mint, sand, periwinkle — never as pure decoration.

The journey is drawn

Progress is a route between stars: dotted trails, orbit paths, badge coins earned at each station. Every screen knows where the traveller came from and what is next.

01 LOGO

The noon wordmark stays untouched — starlight on night, ink on paper. In Guiding Lights it earns one gesture of its own: at reward and milestone moments, the dot may rise as a four-point spark. Everywhere else, the plain mark.

Keep a margin of one “o” width on all sides. Nothing enters it — not even stars.

Never gold text

02 SURFACES

Two skies, one journey

Guiding Lights is a dual-surface system. Night is the sky you navigate; daylight paper is the ground you work on. Screens may be all-night, all-paper, or stacked — but each band commits fully to its surface.

Night الليل

Warm near-black #121015 — never pure black. Starlight lines at low opacity, gold as the single guiding accent.

HeroesJourney mapsMilestonesCelebrationsCovers

Daylight paper النهار

Chalky plaster #EDEAE2 with visible grain. Ink for words; the four pastels label, group, and sort content.

The horizon is the bridge — a line, not a flood. When a screen moves between night and paper, the seam is a gentle arc: paper cresting like a rising planet, traced by one thin coral line and a dashed gold rim. The colour lives in the line; the fields stay night and plaster. Never a hard straight cut.

Jewellery rule: decorative colour — pastels and gold together — holds roughly a tenth of any screen. The large fields always belong to night and plaster; pastels arrive as spines, dots, coins, ribbons and lines. A pastel may fill a full card only on covers and celebration moments, and only one per view. Casting: gold never sits on paper (its foil dies on plaster); text on pastels always uses the deep companion tone, never white.

04 LINE WORK

Drawn by one hand

All celestial drawing shares one pen: even weight, round caps, generous curves. It should feel like an astronomer's plate engraved in gold — precise but warm, never technical wireframe.

R1One weight per plate

Primary strokes 1.5px at 1×; secondary detail 1px. Never mix more than two weights in one drawing.

R2Gold draws, starlight whispers

Named objects (sun, moon, planet, astrolabe) are gold. Ambient sky — waves, stray stars, dotted trails — is starlight at 20–40% opacity.

R3Foil is a reward

The gradient foil fill marks what matters most: one sun, one seal, one earned badge per view. Everything else stays hollow line.

R4Dashes mean movement

Dotted strokes (1 on / 5–7 off) are reserved for routes, orbits and trails — things that travel. Static objects use solid line.

05 ANNOTATION GRAMMAR

Marks in the margin

Small punctuation borrowed from the astronomer's notebook. They sit in margins and corners at 8–16px, in ink on paper or starlight on night — quiet field notes, never the headline.

Spark ✦

Something noteworthy nearby. Max two per view.

Cross ×

A visited waypoint; a plotted point on the map.

Dashed halo

Focus ring; a place not yet reached. Slowly rotates.

Corner brackets

Frames the pinned or selected. Full set of four, or TL+BR pair.

Dot field

Texture patch behind cards and photos. 9px grid, ≤40% opacity.

Zigzag

Hand-ruled divider on paper notes. Short runs only.

07 PATTERN SYSTEM

Four skies to draw on

Patterns are atmosphere, not wallpaper — they sit under content at whisper opacity. Empty Quarter geometry may guest-star here when it earns its place, redrawn in Guiding Lights ink: the khatam becomes an astrolabe rete, the dunes become star-dust waves.

12 MOTION

The sky breathes slowly

Nothing darts. Celestial motion is slow, continuous, and easy to ignore; interface motion is a short soft rise. Respect prefers-reduced-motion everywhere.

Orbit drift

Halos and moons circle at 20–40s per revolution. Ambient only.

Twinkle

Stars fade 15→85% opacity over 3–5s, offset so the sky never pulses in sync.

Soft rise

Content enters with 16px rise + fade, 350–500ms, staggered 60ms. No bounces on paper.

13 KEEP IT TRUE

What Guiding Lights refuses

✕

No pastel floods. Decorative colour is jewellery — pastels and gold together hold about a tenth of any screen. Large fields belong to night and plaster; one full-pastel card per view, covers and celebrations only.

✕

No toy domes. The horizon is a line, not a flood: paper crests like a rising planet, traced by a thin coral line and a dashed gold rim. Big filled colour arcs read as childish.

✕

No drifting into Empty Quarter. Borrowing is deliberate, never accidental: EQ patterns may guest-star once redrawn in Guiding Lights ink (khatam → rete, dunes → star-dust waves). EQ's terracotta palette and semantic roles stay its own.

✕

No neon, no sci-fi. This is the astronomer's night, not cyberspace. No electric greens, no glow-grids, no chrome.

✕

No gold floods. Gold is line and rare foil rewards — never large filled panels, never body text, never on paper surfaces.

✕

No flat untextured pastels. Every pastel surface carries the grain. A smooth vector pastel belongs to another brand.

✕

No decorative Arabic. Arabic leads communication; it is never shrunk to an ornament under English.
